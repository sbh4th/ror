The research question

Overall aim: does the CIHR Project Grant peer review process affect reliability and fairness in scoring and funding success?

  1. Aim 1 – How do reviewer expertise and engagement affect the impact of panel discussion on scores?
  2. Aim 2 – Do these effects differ by applicant gender or career stage?
  3. Aim 3 (exploratory) – How would alternative funding-decision schemes (e.g. weighting by engagement, partial randomization in the grey zone) compare to the status quo?

How does a score become a score?

The process we’re modeling

  • 3 assigned reviewers read the application, score it, discuss, and agree a consensus score
  • All panel members – including the 3 reviewers themselves – then give a final score
  • Final score must be within +/- 0.5 of consensus, entered to one decimal place
  • The average of final scores (equal weight, all members) feeds the funding decision

Confirming our understanding is correct here is one of today’s questions.

Our primary outcome

\[d_{ijk} = \text{final score} - \text{consensus score}\]

for the \(i\)th panel member, \(j\)th application, \(k\)th committee

  • Bounded by design: \(d_{ijk} \in [-0.5, 0.5]\)
  • A member who simply adopts consensus has \(d_{ijk} = 0\) (probably most members, most of the time?)
  • We model the change, not the final score level, because:
    • it matches CIHR’s actual +/- 0.5 rule exactly (an implicit slope of 1, not an estimated one)
    • it keeps “no change” at a fixed value (0) across every application, regardless of where that application’s consensus happened to land

Testing the design before we ask you to run anything

Why we built a simulation first

  • Code has to run correctly remotely, without interactive access to real data
  • So: simulate data with the same structure CIHR would provide, build and pressure-test the full pipeline against it, then hand over code we’re confident in

A constraint we initially missed

  • CIHR scores are entered to one decimal place only – our first simulation draft didn’t enforce this
  • Fixed it, which had a real downstream consequence: the magnitude of deviation among those who do deviate is not continuous – it’s one of 10 discrete steps (+/-0.1 … +/-0.5)

Sharply peaked near +/-0.1, sparse at +/-0.5 – exactly where funding-decision consequences (Aim 3) are largest.

Why not a single linear model on \(d_{ijk}\)?

Checked this empirically on the simulated data before committing to anything more complex:

  • A plain lm(deviation ~ job + exp) finds no meaningful effect of expertise or engagement (largest coefficient: 0.005 points, mostly non-significant)
  • A glm(deviated ~ job + exp, family = binomial) on the same covariates recovers the known, large, highly significant effect built into the simulation
  • Because deviation direction is symmetric around zero, almost all the covariate signal lives in whether someone deviates, not in the average size of the deviation – a mean-only model structurally can’t see it

Model structure

Two linked models (brms has no single native family for “signed, bounded, spike at an interior value”):

Part 1 – did this member deviate at all?

\[\text{deviated}_{ijk} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p_{ijk})\]

Part 2 – if they deviated, by how much?

Ordinal cumulative() model over the 10 discrete steps (not treated as continuous)

\[E[d_{ijk}] = P(\text{deviate}) \times E[\text{magnitude} \mid \text{deviate}]\]

Software

  • Bayesian, via brms + cmdstanr (Stan backend)
  • Same conventions as our other multilevel work: weakly-informative priors, prior-predictive checks before fitting, marginaleffects for contrasts
  • Random effects for committee, reviewer, and application throughout

Questions for the Funding Analytics Team

Which fields can you actually extract?

Fields we’re assuming are available, still to be confirmed:

  • Initial reviewer scores, consensus score, final panel-member scores
  • Self-described expertise, engagement (reviewer/panelist), gender, experience, career stage, past funding success, conflicts of interest
  • Applicant gender, career stage
  • Keywords/domain, re-submission status, PRC, number of investigators, funding requested

Question: which of these are extractable at the panel-member level (not just application level), and linked correctly across the consensus -> final score transition?

Policy changes we need your help accounting for

Year Change

2022 Indigenous Health Research given a separate iterative review process – excluded from general ranking/equalization
